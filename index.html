Full Rocket Assembly

Systems Integration

The Spaceport America Cup Rocketry Project brings together propulsion, avionics, structures, recovery, and manufacturing into a single launch vehicle. A subassembly of the current vehicle is scheduled to fly at FAR Out next year.

My work within Structures Integration spans both design and hands-on manufacturing — understanding how individual structural components work together to transfer loads through the vehicle, then physically building it. SARP / 01

ARES Mount

Mechanical Design · Payload Protection

ARES is a GPS tracking payload used to guide the rocket to a designated recovery point after landing. I designed its mount as part of the vehicle's internal structural architecture, keeping the payload secure and correctly positioned throughout flight and recovery.

The design specifically addresses vibration during flight and tipping on landing, and adds a layer of protection between ARES and the black powder charge used for recovery deployment, isolating the payload's electronics from that event. The mount is 3D printed.

SolidWorks 3D Printing Vibration Payload Protection

SARP / 02

Spar Design & Manufacturing

Structural Design · Manufacturing · Experimental Validation

Designed an aluminum spar located in the vehicle's valve bay, where the airframe is heavily perforated to accommodate valve hardware. With so much material removed from the airframe in this section, the spar serves as the primary load-bearing member and attachment point, carrying loads that the surrounding structure can no longer support on its own.

Because the spar operates under compressive loading in a slender, material-reduced environment, buckling resistance was the dominant design driver. The geometry was sized and iterated using hand calculations and FEA simulations to predict critical buckling loads before committing to manufacturing.

The spar was manufactured on a mill from aluminum stock. To validate the analytical and simulated predictions against real behavior, the finished part was tested in compression on an Instron machine, with the resulting buckling response compared directly against the hand calculations and FEA results.

Aluminum Buckling Analysis FEA Machining Instron Testing

SARP / 03

Bulkheads

Structural Design · Load Transfer

Worked on the vehicle's aluminum bulkheads, including designing one of the bulkheads used to support spar testing. In the valve bay, bulkheads sit above and below the spar, sandwiching it and tying it into the surrounding airframe.

These bulkheads are primarily responsible for transferring motor thrust loads through the vehicle, requiring careful attention to load path, stiffness, and how they interface with the spar and surrounding structure.

04

Helmet Impact Testing

Mechanical Design · Research (In Development)

Designing test equipment toward the NOCSAE and CPSC helmet impact standards as part of an ongoing research project. The system is currently in the design phase and has not yet been built.

I'm responsible for the CAD and mechanical design of the impact pendulum: a swinging arm with an adjustable-weight hammer, raised to an adjustable height by an electromagnet before release. A separate drop test tower, capable of testing at multiple angles, supports additional impact configurations. Impact data captured through accelerometer and gyroscope instrumentation feeds into LS-DYNA simulations to evaluate the resulting forces on the brain.

05

BT-Analyze

Sensor Development · Product Engineering

Independently developed a new handheld skin diagnostic device for Bio-Therapeutics that combines hydration and elasticity measurement — a combination not offered by any existing device on the market. The project began with a review of existing sensing methods before moving into original device and housing design.

The hydration sensor uses a gold-plated interdigitated electrode to take a capacitive reading of skin moisture, while the elasticity sensor uses an indentation force reader to measure ISE. Calibration was difficult, as was maintaining consistent skin contact pressure — solved with a dual-spring loading mechanism. I built the full SolidWorks design and validated the concept through in-house testing; the device itself has not yet been built.

After the initial design was validated, I met with the marketing team to refine the device's look and manufacturability, sourcing suppliers for a custom PCB and smaller components to hit a more market-ready form factor. The final design uses a brushed aluminum case, with the gold-plated interdigitated electrodes mounted directly on the PCB and a stainless steel probe tip on the elasticity side.

SolidWorks Capacitive Sensing Product Design R&D

Sonic Gen2

Product Engineering · Manufacturing

The fix involved switching to a stronger plastic and reworking geometry around the button and tip to relieve stress concentrations at sharp corners and at plastic-to-metal contact points. PERSONAL PROJECT · FABRICATION

A hands-on project combining design, woodworking, fabrication, finishing, precision, and iterative problem solving.

08

Guitar Building

Fabrication · Hands-On Design

Built an electric guitar from the ground up, modeled after a Gibson Les Paul. Starting from raw body and neck blanks, the build included shaping and sanding the body, surface prep, staining, and finishing with tung oil. The neck was sanded and shaped, the fretboard finished with mineral oil, and the headstock decorated.

I soldered the electronics and fit them into the guitar's partial hollow body. Fretwork and the sanding that came with it were the most difficult part of the build, requiring careful, iterative work to get right. One pickup also failed after assembly and had to be diagnosed and resoldered before the guitar was fully functional.
